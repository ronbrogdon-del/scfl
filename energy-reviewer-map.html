Where SCFL maps in energy

The Standard Coherence Fidelity Layer (SCFL) is a structural-state measurement instrument. It reads the condition of the joins between systems—the seams—like an inspection rather than an alarm. This map shows where it applies across energy segments, what has been tested, what is planned, and which open problems a grant would answer.

Rows and columns: Energy segments. A cell is the seam where two segments meet; the diagonal is a seam inside one segment.

Colour: What exists today, labelled honestly, negative results included.

Click a cell: Scope, papers and links for each result, the open question, and the programs it serves.

The lead intersect: gas–electric

Gas-fired plants supply a large share of ERCOT’s power, and Winter Storm Uri showed what happens when gas fails to reach them. Yet the seam between pipeline deliveries and gas-fired generation is largely unmeasured: historical observed deliveries to plants are not publicly available, and ERCOT has worked since 2021 to gather gas operations and delivery information.

Our path:

  1. Now: instrument verification on a coupled synthetic testbed (Texas A&M combined electric–gas test case), injecting gas shortfalls of known size and timing, with a no-shortfall control band and pass criteria fixed before any run.
  2. Funded phase: a pre-registered test of the precursor window on observed gas-delivery and generation data, run on the frozen engine and published whatever the result.

Instrument: SCFL engine v1.1.0, frozen 31 October 2026 (freeze record, 10.5281/zenodo.23839571). Results labelled “pre-freeze” were produced before the freeze and are listed as historical record, each scoped to its own question; they are not pooled as evidence for one another.
