Program Overview & Mission

The UCMS Request to Measure (RTM) Research Program invites facility managers, national laboratories, and academic institutions to collaborate on non-invasive, multi-domain system characterization.

Using proprietary internal operators, UCMS evaluates stress-event coherence, operational recoverability, and system health from raw structural, energy, and environmental telemetry. Our operational framework requires zero proprietary software or algorithm installation on host systems. Host partners provide raw time-series exports; UCMS performs computation internally and returns actionable diagnostic insights.

Collaboration Framework

  • Data Security & IP Protection: Partners maintain full ownership of all raw data exports. UCMS proprietary operators and formulas remain internal, avoiding complex IP disputes or network integration hurdles.
  • Participation Tiers:
    1. Retrospective Data Analysis: Diagnostic evaluation of historical telemetry covering documented stress events.
    2. Controlled Testbed Runs: Evaluation during scheduled, imposed-disturbance testing at research facilities.
    3. Live System Monitoring: Continuous or episodic analysis on active, occupied campuses and commercial sites.

That outcome is acceptable. A bounded engagement is credible only if it can return an inconclusive or negative result rather than manufacture a positive finding. Stopping conditions are defined before execution.

Important clarification: The academic pathways described (undergraduate capstone, graduate project, faculty-sponsored program) are options for structuring research, not established university partnerships or accredited programs. Any actual engagement requires separate institutional agreement.

Methodology

A Request to Measure engagement follows a bounded, documented process. The goal is to produce interpretable structural measurements under controlled conditions so that an institution can independently assess whether the outputs contribute information beyond an agreed baseline.

1. Scope Definition

  • One authorized dataset
  • One primary research question
  • One defined observation window or experimental scenario
  • One pre-agreed comparison baseline, where feasible
  • Explicit stopping conditions and deliverables

Scope is fixed before execution. Expansion is a separate decision, not an automatic continuation.

2. Data Handling

  • The institution retains ownership of its raw data.
  • Accepted formats include CSV, Parquet, HDF5, and MiniSEED.
  • Partners may redact equipment identifiers, site names, or locations.
  • Required metadata: sensor type, units, timestamp sequence, and relative layout where relevant.
  • For blind evaluation, event timestamps, fault logs, and maintenance records are withheld until after the independent report is delivered.

3. Execution

  1. Dataset transfer via secure method agreed by both parties.
  2. Controlled execution of the frozen measurement specification (no source-code transfer required).
  3. Internal processing produces structural state outputs, temporal observations where supported, and integrity checks.
  4. Delivery of an independent report identifying findings, coverage, and limitations.
  5. Optional unblinding and comparison against institutional ground truth.

4. Outputs

The minimum package includes:

  • Measurement report (question, scope, configuration, findings, limitations)
  • Structural state and change profile
  • Coverage and evidence register
  • Baseline comparison, where feasible
  • Method and integrity record (specification version, configuration identifier, execution date)
  • Decision brief stating what was established and what was not

5. Independent Assessment

Researchers may examine the data, select baselines, analyze authorized outputs, and report findings under the agreed protocol. Negative or inconclusive results are acceptable outcomes. The measurement is evaluated against pre-agreed criteria rather than against a predetermined conclusion.

Real-Time and Near Real-Time Perspective Cases

The majority of the dozens of prior test cases have been historical and retrospective. Those reconstructions have been useful for method development, but the current need is for real-time or near real-time perspective cases.

Why this matters: Historical analysis can show that structural change was readable in recorded data. Real-time or near real-time engagements test whether the same measurement can be produced while the system is still operating, under the latency, data quality, and decision constraints that institutions actually face.

What a real-time or near real-time engagement tests

  • Whether the measurement can be executed on current or recent telemetry rather than archived records alone
  • Whether outputs remain interpretable under ordinary operational data conditions
  • Whether the institution can receive and evaluate results on a timeline that is useful for its own research or operational questions
  • How the measurement behaves when the observation window is still open rather than closed and fully documented

Relationship to historical work

Historical cases remain valuable for establishing baseline behavior, stress-event response, and method integrity. They do not, by themselves, answer whether the measurement can be produced prospectively. Real-time or near real-time cases are therefore a distinct and currently higher-priority class of engagement.

A bounded Request to Measure pilot can be designed as either a retrospective analysis of an existing record or a near real-time run on an active data stream. Both are valid. The program now explicitly seeks the latter where suitable datasets and institutional partners exist.

A bounded, in-situ SCFL Measurement Package for universities. The university supplies or identifies an existing dataset, the measurement process runs within its own environment, and the university receives a defined set of structural observations and outputs. You retain control of the calculation engine.

Important distinction: This is not a request to adopt SCFL as a theory, validate an entire research program, or build software. It is a limited, controlled measurement engagement on one dataset, one question, and one execution.

The Package

The university provides

  • An existing dataset
  • A designated research or technical contact
  • A secure computing environment
  • Permission to analyze an agreed period or population of observations

SCFL provides

  • A bounded measurement protocol
  • Approved data mapping
  • Controlled execution of the protected operators
  • A defined output specification

The university receives

  • A structural measurement report
  • Time-resolved observations where supported
  • Identified changes in measured relationships
  • Limitations statement
  • A reproducible record of what was actually measured

The university can examine the outputs without receiving operator definitions or the internal calculation sequence. In-situ measurement does not require transferring ownership of the method.

Proposed Pilot Specification (Deliberately Narrow)

  • One dataset — One agreed asset, process, cohort, or system; no open-ended access to the university’s entire research estate.
  • One measurement question — For example: Can an existing dataset reveal material changes in relational structure that conventional aggregate indicators do not describe?
  • One bounded observation window — A defined historical interval or prospective period, selected according to the data’s temporal resolution.
  • One controlled execution — Operators run in a controlled environment or restricted execution service. Raw data and intermediate calculations need not leave the university.
  • One final deliverable — Measurement report, output files in agreed formats, methods-and-limitations statement, and a record sufficient to reproduce or audit the run to the extent contractually agreed.

A reasonable initial scope is one dataset, one question, one execution, and one report. Duration is set after the dataset is identified.

What the University Sees (Inspectable Evidence)

The deliverable is more than a graph or a single score. Specified outputs include:

  • Measurement coverage: what was measured, time span, sampling resolution, missing data, exclusions, and limitations imposed by the source data.
  • Structural state outputs: the agreed SCFL measurements and state characterizations, with units, scales, and definitions for the reported outputs.
  • Temporal record: changes in those measurements over time, where the data supports time-resolved analysis.
  • Change-point or event annotations: where material changes appear in the measured structure, without automatically claiming they predict failure.
  • Conventional-indicator comparison: where feasible, comparison against existing indicators or operating records to show what the additional measurement contributes.
  • Interpretation and limitations: what the results support, what remains ambiguous, and what the run cannot establish.
  • Technical execution record: dataset version or fingerprint, configuration identifier, run date, exclusions, and output integrity checks.

The meaning of reported measurements can be disclosed without disclosing the proprietary procedure used to calculate them. The boundary is deliberate: proprietary computation remains internal; evidence remains inspectable.

Suitable University Datasets

Initial preference is for engineering or infrastructure datasets. Examples:

Campus infrastructure

Building-management systems, chilled-water plants, electrical loads, HVAC telemetry, pump systems, and other instrumented facilities.

Civil & structural engineering

University Research Program Models

The bounded SCFL measurement package can become the basis of a university research program that uses existing datasets to evaluate a new measurement capability without requiring students or faculty to receive proprietary operators.

Three possible models offer different benefits and different levels of institutional commitment.

1. Undergraduate Capstone or Senior Design

Typical duration: one academic semester

Students work with an approved dataset and a tightly specified measurement task. They examine data quality, prepare comparison baselines, interpret authorized SCFL outputs, and assess whether the results add useful information.

Student deliverables: technical report, reproducible baseline analysis, comparison of results, and presentation to faculty.

Benefit: low-commitment way to establish an initial university relationship and test the usability of the output package.

2. Graduate Research Project

Typical duration: one or more semesters

A master's student or doctoral researcher investigates a more demanding question: whether SCFL measurements discriminate among different structural conditions, remain stable under specified data perturbations, or reveal changes not captured by conventional indicators.

Research deliverables: formal protocol, controlled comparisons, sensitivity analysis, documented limitations, and potentially a thesis or paper.

Benefit: stronger independent scrutiny and potentially more credible evidence than a demonstration alone.

The research must be designed so that students can evaluate the claims without needing unrestricted access to the proprietary operators.

3. Faculty-Sponsored Research Program

Typical duration: multiple semesters or years

A faculty member or laboratory hosts a continuing program in structural measurement, using SCFL as a defined research instrument across carefully selected datasets.

Program deliverables: multiple studies, documented protocols, independent assessment, and a growing body of results.

Benefit: possibility of a continuing research relationship, a credible external research record, and future commercial applications.

This model requires more commitment, governance, and potentially funding, so it is pursued after a successful initial pilot.

What Students Can Study Without Receiving Operators

This is the key to making the program academically legitimate. Students investigate questions that can be answered independently of the protected calculations:

  • Incremental information: Does the output provide information that the dataset’s conventional indicators do not already convey?
  • Discrimination: Does it distinguish between known structural conditions and appropriate comparison cases?
  • Robustness: How sensitive are the findings to missing observations, sampling choices, or permitted data perturbations?
  • Temporal interpretation: Do changes in the measurements correspond to documented changes in the system?
  • Reproducibility: Can the same authorized inputs and configuration reproduce the reported outputs?
  • Limitations: Under what conditions is the measurement uninformative, ambiguous, or inappropriate?

These questions create real student research rather than a marketing exercise. A negative or inconclusive result is an acceptable outcome.

Intellectual-Property Boundary

  • University dataset: Existing authorized data and documented reference conditions
  • Protected SCFL execution: Controlled measurement engine; no source-code transfer required
  • Research outputs: Approved measurements, comparison data, limitations, and execution records
  • Student and faculty investigation: Independent analysis of what the measurements show and do not show

The operator implementation is retained. Students receive enough output documentation to interpret the measurements and test agreed claims. Any publication arrangement must preserve academic integrity while protecting genuinely confidential implementation details. Restrictions that prevent students from completing a thesis or publishing required findings must be negotiated upfront.

Preferred Starting Route

Start with one faculty-sponsored undergraduate capstone, with a graduate researcher or faculty adviser overseeing the methodology if available.

  1. Identify one faculty member with an appropriate existing dataset.
  2. Agree on one bounded research question and the comparison baseline.
  3. Execute the protected measurement and provide the defined outputs.
  4. Let the students independently assess those outputs.
  5. Produce a technical report and, if the evidence warrants it, a jointly agreed public methods note.
  6. Use the result to seek a funded graduate project or continuing research partnership.

This provides an entry point without establishing a permanent laboratory, hiring a research team, or disclosing the frozen operator set at the outset.

Note: A capstone is not automatically a funded research engagement. Some projects require a university fee, a sponsored-project agreement, or an industry liaison; others may be undertaken without a sponsor. The exact arrangement depends on the institution.

Recommended framing: Approach this as a sponsored applied-research opportunity, not as a request for students to build SCFL. Supply the measurement capability and bounded research question; the university supplies the research environment and independent scrutiny.

Institutional Administration Model

Full discussion document: Rules of Engagement & Institutional Research Program Q&A (Institutional Discussion Draft)

The founder should not become the administrator of a distributed research program on top of being the originator of SCFL, its principal intellectual contributor, its commercial advocate, and the person responsible for protecting its operators. That would create a bottleneck. Design the structure so that intellectual stewardship is retained while an established institution assumes responsibility for research-program administration.

1. Roles

You — Founder and Scientific Originator

  • Stewardship of the SCFL/UCMS framework and frozen operators
  • Definition of measurement boundaries and permitted use
  • Approval of authorized changes to the measurement specification
  • Strategic partnerships, commercialization, and intellectual-property oversight

Host Institution — Program Administrator

  • Faculty recruitment and project allocation
  • Research agreements, budgets, and administrative coordination
  • Student supervision, research ethics, and institutional compliance
  • Dataset intake, project scheduling, records, and reporting
  • Publication workflows and coordination with participating institutions

Participating Laboratories and Students

  • Supply or identify suitable datasets
  • Execute approved research protocols
  • Conduct comparison analyses and independent assessments
  • Produce technical reports, theses, and eligible publications

The host does not necessarily need to own SCFL. It administers the research program under an agreement that defines its authority, responsibilities, and limits.

2. What Kind of Facility to Seek

Seek an existing unit that already administers external research collaborations, not an informal departmental favor.

  • Interdisciplinary research institute (preferred): already coordinates faculty across engineering, systems science, computing, and other disciplines.
  • University applied-research center: particularly suitable for instrumented assets, industrial data, energy systems, or structural integrity.
  • Sponsored-research or research-services office: manages agreements, budgets, and compliance; may need scientific leadership alongside it.
  • Independent nonprofit research institute: useful for a continuing, cross-university program.

The administrator and the scientific host can be different organizations. An institute can manage the program while individual university laboratories perform the research.

3. Bounded Administrative Mandate

Create a small, explicitly bounded administrative mandate rather than asking an institution to adopt the whole SCFL research agenda. The host would be responsible for:

  • Establishing a research-program coordinator role
  • Managing participating university relationships
  • Maintaining a project register and standard agreements
  • Coordinating dataset screening and project selection
  • Administering any sponsored funding
  • Maintaining research records and coordinating agreed reporting
  • Organizing independent technical review and publication processes

You remain responsible for the intellectual boundaries of SCFL, but not the daily administration of individual projects. Administrative scope, staffing, funding, authority, liability, and intellectual-property arrangements must be agreed explicitly.

4. How to Make This Attractive to a Host

Frame the opportunity as the establishment of an administered, multi-project applied-research program in upstream structural measurement. The institution is not asked to validate all of SCFL in advance. It is invited to determine whether the proposed program merits hosting and to begin with one bounded demonstration.

The initial proposal requests:

  • An institutional sponsor or faculty champion
  • An administrative home and designated coordinator
  • One suitable dataset and research group
  • A defined pilot budget, including administrative costs
  • A governance agreement separating program administration from proprietary measurement implementation
  • A decision point at which the institution and founder assess whether to continue

5. Funding

An institution may be willing to host research but unwilling to administer a continuing program without funding. Make administrative capacity part of the initial proposal. Potential structures include a sponsored-research agreement, a foundation grant, a program-related charitable grant where eligible, or an external sponsor paying the institution’s agreed administrative and research costs. The host’s fee and founder compensation or licensing arrangements should be separately specified and transparent.

6. Strategic Consequence

A cross-domain research program cannot depend indefinitely on one person personally coordinating every dataset, student, faculty member, agreement, and report. The structure needs to become transferable even while the measurement method remains protected.

Institutional proposition: The founder provides the measurement framework and retains intellectual stewardship. An established research institution administers the program, coordinates independent investigators, and manages the research process. Participating laboratories evaluate bounded applications under agreed protocols.

The immediate objective is to find one institution willing to take responsibility for the program, rather than merely lend a dataset. The next practical step is a two-page Host Institution Proposal that defines the administrative mandate, minimum staffing and funding, the first pilot, governance boundaries, and what the institution gains from hosting the program.

Blind Real-Time & Retrospective Testing Requirements

Document Reference: RTM-REQ-2026-01

Inquiry Type: Blind Diagnostic Capabilities Evaluation

1. Protocol Purpose

This protocol establishes the operational terms for a Blind Evaluation. UCMS processes raw, unlabelled time-series data provided by the host facility without prior knowledge of internal fault logs or event timestamps. The goal is to independently detect, isolate, and characterize system state transitions and anomalies.

2. Data Requirements & Anonymization

  • Accepted Formats: CSV, Parquet, HDF5, MiniSEED.
  • Anonymization: Partners may redact exact equipment IDs, site names, or GPS locations.
  • Provided Metadata: Sensor type, physical units, timestamp sequence (UTC/local), relative layout.
  • Withheld Information (Blind Validation): Specific event timestamps, fault logs, or maintenance records.

3. Execution Steps

  1. Transfer: Partner transmits dataset via secure file transfer.
  2. Internal Processing: UCMS executes proprietary calculations offline.
  3. Reporting: UCMS delivers an independent report identifying event timestamps and coherence metrics.
  4. Unblinding & Review: Results are compared against partner ground-truth operational logs.
