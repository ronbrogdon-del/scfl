Problem spaces

Questions that reveal what conventional measures miss.

A structured set of human-flourishing questions that expose the relationships, constraints, and conditions that allow people and human systems to function, adapt, recover, and flourish — or that quietly erode those capacities while conventional indicators still look acceptable.

Organizing principle

Outcomes, or the conditions that sustain them?

The central distinction: Are we measuring the outcomes people currently produce, or the underlying conditions that allow them to continue functioning and flourishing?

A workforce can meet its targets while losing trust, discretionary cooperation, and the ability to recover from sustained pressure. A community can maintain services while its social connections weaken. An individual can remain outwardly productive while losing the internal relationships that sustain a coherent sense of direction.

These are candidate problem spaces. They are not yet proof that the measurement already captures every condition. Each question is meant to identify a consequential human problem, expose something conventional measures may miss, and open a path toward an observable, testable proposition.

Eight problem spaces

Where the conditions for flourishing can be examined.

1. Human flourishing at work

Can people continue to flourish in organizations that appear to be performing well?

  • Can people raise concerns without damaging their standing?
  • Do working relationships enable recovery from conflict and disruption?
  • Are performance targets being maintained at the expense of the conditions that sustain performance?
  • Does cooperation survive pressure, or does each department retreat into self-protection?

Measurement opportunity: relationships between organizational performance, cooperation, internal constraints, and recoverability.

2. Interior coherence and personal flourishing

What allows a person to retain a coherent inner life amid competing demands, uncertainty, and change?

  • Can a person reconcile conflicting responsibilities without losing a sense of direction?
  • Do their commitments, values, relationships, and daily actions remain mutually supportive?
  • When life becomes difficult, what helps restore a sense of integration?
  • Can a person change profoundly without losing their sense of continuity?

Measurement opportunity: nonclinical exploration of relationships among personal commitments, perceived direction, and capacity to regain integration. Distinct from clinical diagnosis.

3. Family relationships and human development

What enables human relationships to remain supportive through conflict, stress, and change?

  • Can family members disagree without threatening their underlying bond?
  • Are people able to repair relationships after a rupture?
  • Do children have dependable relationships that allow exploration, autonomy, and growth?
  • What happens when the demands placed on a family exceed its capacity to adapt?

Measurement opportunity: relational continuity, repair, mutual support, and the conditions associated with healthy development.

4. Community belonging and social connection

What allows a community to remain connected when its members face competing interests and shared adversity?

  • Do people retain meaningful connections across differences?
  • Can local institutions coordinate when circumstances change?
  • Are relationships strong enough to support collective action beyond a crisis?
  • Does apparent social stability conceal declining participation, trust, or reciprocity?

Measurement opportunity: connections among participation, trust, institutional coordination, and collective recoverability.

5. Healthcare and human care systems

Can a care system preserve the relationships necessary for good care under sustained pressure?

  • Can clinicians communicate across professional boundaries when workloads increase?
  • Does continuity of care survive staff turnover and resource constraints?
  • Can patients and caregivers remain meaningfully connected to decisions?
  • Does the system preserve its ability to recover from disruption, rather than merely clear its backlog?

Measurement opportunity: relational continuity and coordination across people, teams, and care transitions, alongside existing clinical and operational measures.

6. Leadership, governance, and institutional trust

What allows institutions to retain the internal capacity to function when authority, incentives, and reality come into conflict?

  • Can unfavorable information reach decision-makers without distortion?
  • Do incentives support the institution's stated purpose, or undermine it?
  • Can departments resolve conflicting objectives before those conflicts become operational failures?
  • Does an institution still have the capacity to correct itself when its usual assumptions stop working?

Measurement opportunity: cross-boundary coherence, information integrity, competing constraints, and organizational self-correction.

7. Education, learning, and human potential

Do our learning environments develop the conditions people need to grow, adapt, and realize their potential?

  • Can learners integrate new knowledge rather than simply accumulate information?
  • Do relationships among students, educators, families, and institutions reinforce learning?
  • Can a learner recover from failure without losing engagement or a sense of possibility?
  • Do institutional performance measures reflect durable development or primarily short-term attainment?

Measurement opportunity: relationships among learning, engagement, support, adaptation, and persistence over time.

8. Civilizational flourishing

What allows a society to preserve the relationships and institutions on which its long-term flourishing depends?

  • Can economic growth coexist with declining social trust or institutional legitimacy?
  • Do institutions retain the capacity to coordinate across competing interests?
  • Can a society absorb shocks without permanently losing essential capabilities?
  • Are the conditions supporting future generations being preserved even when current indicators remain favorable?

Measurement opportunity: structural relationships among institutions, infrastructure, economic activity, social coordination, and recoverability. The broadest and most demanding domain.

The Flourishing Question Set

Cross-cutting questions conventional measures are not designed to ask.

01 · HIDDEN DEGRADATION

Can a system appear healthy while losing the conditions necessary to remain healthy?

The question behind apparently successful organizations, families, institutions, and communities whose underlying relationships may be deteriorating.

02 · RELATIONAL INTEGRITY

What relationships must remain intact for people to function and flourish together?

Attention beyond isolated individuals, outcomes, and performance indicators toward the relationships that support them.

03 · RECOVERABILITY

What determines whether people and organizations can regain their footing after disruption?

Surviving a difficult event is not the same as retaining the capacity to recover from it.

04 · COMPETING DEMANDS

When does the pressure to satisfy one objective begin to undermine the relationships required to satisfy others?

Productivity versus well-being, growth versus institutional integrity, individual incentives versus collective purpose.

05 · INVISIBLE COSTS

What is being depleted today that will be needed tomorrow?

Trust, cooperation, continuity, mutual support, and the ability to coordinate may deteriorate before conventional performance measures reveal the cost.

06 · ADAPTATION

Can a system change without destroying the relationships that make it functional?

Flourishing requires more than stability. It also requires the capacity to adapt while preserving what matters.

07 · EARLY RECOGNITION

Can measurable changes in relational structure be detected before consequential human or operational outcomes change?

A testable research proposition, not an established capability across every domain.

08 · HUMAN FLOURISHING

What are the underlying conditions that allow human beings to develop, sustain meaningful relationships, adapt, and continue flourishing over time?

The overarching question that connects the entire program.

From questions to offerings

Prioritized entry points.

Not every important human question is something the measurement can already address. Each requires a disciplined progression from problem to observable variables to validated measurement.

First: Organizational flourishing

The clearest initial commercial entry point. Examine whether organizational relationships, cross-functional coordination, and recoverability are consistent with continued performance. Potential buyers include enterprise leadership, operational risk, and selected insurers and reinsurers.

Second: Human flourishing research

A distinct, nonclinical research program around interior coherence, meaningful direction, relational integration, and adaptation. Attracts research collaborators and mission-aligned funders; requires its own instruments and validation.

Third: Healthcare, education, and communities

Investigate whether relational measures contribute information beyond established indicators of care continuity, learning, participation, or institutional performance. Begin with a narrowly defined research partner and outcome.

Fourth: Civilizational flourishing

Retain as the broad intellectual horizon and a potential long-term research program. Its scale makes it a less suitable starting point for a first commercial engagement.

Next step

A usable question bank.

Each question should carry five associated elements: the human problem (what can go wrong, and for whom), the blind spot (what current measures may fail to capture), the relational hypothesis (what underlying relationships might matter), the observable evidence (what data could actually be collected), and the practical value (who would benefit from knowing the answer, and what decision might change).

This supports a growing library of problem spaces, candidate measures, and potential research or commercial engagements without claiming that every question has already been solved.

The organizing purpose is human flourishing. Coherence is the proposed underlying construct. SCFL is the measurement approach being developed to investigate it. Flourishing is not reduced to organizational productivity, psychological comfort, or the absence of crisis. The larger question is whether the conditions for meaningful, sustained human development remain intact.
