Primary Domain Plate · Funding Opportunity

Energy: the largest coherence measurement research questions.

The Standard Coherence Fidelity Layer (SCFL) is a structural-state measurement instrument. It characterizes the condition of the relationships that hold a system together—including seams, internal constraints, and remaining margin relative to demand—and is designed to read whether loss of recoverability is present while conventional indicators are still stationary.

Energy systems are the most fully instrumented, highest-stakes, and most publicly documented coupled systems available for upstream coherence measurement. They are the primary domain plate for the SCFL / UCMS research program and the clearest funding opportunity for domain plates, institutional pilots, and cross-sector collaboration.

The same structural questions appear across generation, transmission, distribution, markets, and the oil & gas value chain. Conventional monitoring watches components and outputs. The research questions ask whether the relationships that hold the system together remain intact under stress — while those conventional indicators are still stationary — and whether loss of recoverability is readable before outcomes move.

The biggest coherence measurement research questions in energy

Recoverability · Precursor geometry

Is loss of recoverability readable while conventional indicators remain stationary?

The EAGLE-I national outage corpus and ERCOT stress-event plates show precursor geometry with measurable lead times (mean 8.9 h, range 1–71 h in the national set) before operator-visible disturbances. The Uri reconstruction documents a five-minute window between irreversible cascade and grid failure, with a larger upstream window. The open question is whether this geometry generalizes across regions, resource mixes, and market structures, and what operator decision rules it supports.

See: Before the Alarm · SCFL on ERCOT stress events · Uri Precursor Window · Uri and Before the Alarm videos

Seams · Cross-operator structure

What is the structural state of the seams no single operator sees?

Generation, transmission, distribution, and markets are operated by different entities. Interconnection queues, market clearing versus physical constraints, and gen–trans–dist interfaces are seams. The research question is whether relational structure across these boundaries can be characterized from existing data, and whether seam shear is readable while each operator’s own metrics remain inside tolerance. Enron is the public reconstruction of market and financial layers shearing from physical energy flows.

See: Enron reconstruction · America’s Grid Is Running Out of Runway · Across boundaries

Hidden erosion · Margin

Is structural margin being consumed while output and reliability metrics still hold?

Declining synchronous inertia, aging assets, deferred maintenance, rising extreme-weather liabilities, and capital stress can reduce recoverability without immediately moving conventional performance numbers. The question is whether this consumption of margin is measurable as a change in relational structure, and how it interacts with resource-mix transitions and large-load growth.

See: Hidden erosion · Grid white paper · EAGLE-I and ERCOT plates

Lifecycle · As-built

Do new builds, retirements, and repairs restore structure or only capacity?

Interconnection queues now hold more capacity than the installed fleet, with most projects withdrawing. Transmission projects take a decade. Thermal and nuclear retirements change system geometry. The research question is whether lifecycle checkpoints (queue entry, interconnection, retirement, post-event repair) can be read for structural restoration versus capacity restoration, using the dated records that already exist.

See: Lifecycle checkpoints · LBNL queue data referenced in the grid white paper

Localization · Seams in motion

Where in the network is recoverability being lost, and do seams hold as boundaries shift?

Data-center load growth, renewable penetration, storage, DERs, and electrification move system boundaries while the system is under load. The question is whether the location of recoverability loss can be localized, and whether moving seams remain coherent under the new load geometry.

See: Localization · Seams in motion

Why energy is the primary domain plate

Energy systems already generate the telemetry SCFL reads. They have public outage records, market data, and post-event investigations. The same questions apply across segments:

Generation

Inertia, resource mix, weather correlation, retirements.

Transmission

Queues, congestion, interregional seams.

Distribution & utilities

Aging assets, DERs, extreme-weather exposure.

Markets & RTOs/ISOs

Price signals versus physical deliverability.

Oil & gas value chain

Physical flows versus contractual and financial layers.

Emerging interfaces

Storage, large loads, shifting boundaries.

Fit is decided by requirements, not industry label: interdependent parts, dated records of those parts and their relationships, and a decision that depends on remaining margin.
